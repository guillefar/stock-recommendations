**Session 26 fixed a measurement bug that the feedback loop had been amplifying into production prompts** — **merged to `main` @ `c06c89b`** on 2026-08-03. It began as a production audit after ~2.5 weeks of unattended running, and the audit is the headline.

**Audit finding 1 — the grading bands were miscalibrated for ETFs, and every top mined pattern was an artifact of it.** `HORIZON_BANDS` are *single-stock* volatility bands (the 30d `watch_move` of 10% sits on the measured median 30d stock move of 10.7%) but were applied to every instrument. Measured: an ETF's median 30d move is **2.62%** (mean absolute 4.29%) against a stock's **10.70%** (14.67%), and only **13.6%** of ETF observations ever travel the 10% a WATCH needs to score CORRECT while **64.5%** sit inside the 4% neutral band that scores a WATCH INCORRECT. So `WATCH×ETF` was arithmetically required to fail — its 344 INCORRECT rows averaged a **1.66%** move — and `HOLD×ETF` was required to succeed, because HOLD is CORRECT when flat and ETFs are flat. The pattern miner had duly discovered both, promoted them to CONFIRMED at 0.91–0.95 across three Fridays, and **since Mon 07-20 the s25 loop had been injecting them into all 63 ticker prompts** to suppress WATCH on ETFs. The system was being taught a bug. `ASSET_CLASS_BAND_SCALE = {"ETF": 0.30}` fixes it: the factor is the measured ETF/stock mean-absolute-move ratio, which is **stable across horizons** (0.317 at 7d, 0.292 at 30d), so one factor serves every horizon and preserves the base bands' √time shape and monotonicity. New `bands_for(horizon, quote_type)`; `grade()` takes an optional 4th `quote_type` arg (default None → unscaled, so the index and untyped tickers are unchanged and every pre-session-26 test still holds).

**The re-grade (user-approved) landed and matched the pre-flight simulation almost exactly**: `WATCH×ETF` **15.1% → 59.6%** (vs `WATCH×STOCK` 67.4%), `HOLD×ETF` **94.5% → 62.7%** (vs `HOLD×STOCK` 50.7%), overall 30d **51.9% → 60.5%**, 7d 59.1% → 66.5%. ## Decisions log

- _2026-08-19 (session 31)_ — **The scorecard's headline is excess over a null, not a hit rate — and the null is a permutation, not a simulation.** Session 30 established that skill-free calls score 63–67% under this scheme; session 31 pins the number by shuffling the actions *between real corpus rows* and re-grading, which holds the action mix, the realized returns, the market regime and the per-instrument bands fixed and varies only the association between a call and its outcome. That is the definition of skill, so it is the right null, and it reproduces the out-of-corpus figure independently at **65.8%** (30d). Two shuffles, because they answer different questions: **global** (actions moved anywhere) gives the headline **+2.7pp**; **within-ticker** (instrument selection held fixed) gives **−2.3pp**. The decomposition of the 68.5% is therefore: 65.8 from chance, **+5.0pp from pairing calm instruments with HOLD and volatile ones with WATCH**, and **−2.3pp from timing**. The system's measurable edge is which instruments it assigns which action to, and it is not a stock-picking edge — it is roughly the same information the per-instrument band table already encodes. **`WATCH` is the cleanest result: excess ~0.0pp under every specification tried, while being 51% of all calls.** The dashboard now leads with the excess, keeps the raw rate as a grey tile, and draws the null as a reference line; `check_run.py` prints the same three numbers into every CI run summary. **Standing rule, now enforced in two places rather than asserted in prose: never render a hit rate without its null adjacent to it.** Note the null must be re-derived whenever the bands or the action mix change — a stale null silently turns the excess tile back into a hit rate.
- _2026-08-19 (session 31)_ — **A permutation over overlapping windows is not a significance test, and the session's most exciting finding died on that point.** Against the global null `SELL` scored **+27.0pp (+5.1 sd)** and read as the one action with genuine skill; against the within-ticker null the identical calls scored **−7.3pp**. The sign flips with the choice of null — exactly session 30's lesson, recurring inside a single session and on a statistic built *to apply* that lesson. Separately, recommendations are generated Mon/Wed/Fri while the horizon is 30 days, so consecutive rows for one ticker share ~93% of their forward window: the ~2,400 rows at 30d carry closer to **~110 independent observations**, and a permutation that treats them as independent produces a null distribution that is too narrow and z-values that are inflated throughout. The script therefore ships a `--non-overlapping` mode (keep rows a full horizon apart, resample, and **pool counts rather than average per-draw rates**, which is biased when a slice holds ~5 rows), labels the column `sd*`, and states in its own footer which of its numbers survive every specification and which are hypotheses. **`BUY` is negative under all three nulls (−17.7 / −20.2 / −33.6pp) and is the only per-action sign that never moves — but it rests on ~12 independent calls and is recorded as a hypothesis for session 32, not a finding.** Generalisation: **when you build a tool whose purpose is to stop other numbers being over-read, the first number to audit is the tool's own.**
- _2026-08-17 (session 30)_ — **A hit rate without a null baseline is not evidence, and this one never had one.** Grading skill-free, randomly generated calls over a year entirely outside the corpus yields **63–67%** under this scheme. The system's re-graded headline is **68.8%** and its previous one was **60%** — i.e. the number the scorecard has carried since session 10 sits within noise of what dice would score, and the pre-re-grade figure was *below* it. The cause is structural rather than a bug: `HOLD` grades CORRECT when the price stays flat and `WATCH` grades CORRECT when it moves, and those two actions carry **89%** of all calls, so nearly any outcome lands on the good side of something. **This does not show the system lacks skill; it shows the headline cannot answer the question in either direction.** Standing rule, and the third restatement of the same lesson in five sessions: before quoting an aggregate, compute what it would read if the finding were false. Fixing it is the pinned next slice — a pinned null reference line on the scorecard is the cheap version, benchmark-relative grading the principled one.
- _2026-08-17 (session 30)_ — **Grading bands scale per instrument, from a window that ends before the corpus.** Session 26 scaled by asset class, session 28 found the class field had gaps, and session 30 found the class itself is the wrong unit: implied scales span **0.087–0.766 within ETFs** (8.8×) and **0.286–2.688 within equities** (9.4×), and the classes overlap so heavily that four ETFs move further than AAPL. At the edges the yardstick decided the verdict before the call was read — `^STOXX50E` needed a 10% move for a CORRECT WATCH and moves 1.56%, scoring **0 CORRECT out of 22 ever**, while `SPY5.PA` moves 0.57% against a 1.2% "flat" band and collected **17 free CORRECTs**. One auto-fails and one auto-passes; both are the same bug, and it is the one session 26 set out to remove. `bands_for` now resolves **instrument → class → unscaled**, so the class constant survives only as the fallback for a ticker too new to estimate. **The estimation window ending 2026-05-16 is the load-bearing constraint, not a detail**: bands fitted to the returns they grade would flatten every hit rate by construction and use the future to score the past. Validated out of sample twice — Spearman **0.90** against what the corpus actually did, and persistence across two disjoint prior years at **R² 0.83, slope 1.18**. That slope is also why **no shrinkage was applied**: none is warranted, and calibrating one against this corpus would have reintroduced precisely the circularity the window exists to avoid. **5,244 rows re-graded** (user-approved, simulated, read back at 0 mismatches); 30d headline 60.0% → **68.8%** — which is exactly why the null-baseline entry above matters more than this one.
- _2026-08-17 (session 30)_ — **Compare a suspicious statistic against its own null, not against zero.** The per-instrument yardstick showed rho **−0.197** between an instrument's volatility and its hit rate, against **−0.029** for the class yardstick, and the obvious reading — that the new scheme had introduced a bias — was wrong and nearly blocked the slice. Under skill-free calls the two yardsticks have *different* null rhos: **+0.140** for asset class and **+0.045** for per-instrument. Measured against their own baselines the per-instrument scheme is the less biased of the two, and the corpus figure sits 1.3 sd from its null. The near-zero **−0.029** that made the old yardstick look clean was two opposite-direction artifacts cancelling: `^STOXX50E` calm and scoring 0%, `SPY5.PA` calm and scoring 92%. **Corollary: a correlation is the wrong instrument for detecting an artifact that can point either way — use spread.** Two further hypotheses for the residual tilt were tested and discarded (volatility mean-reversion, contradicted by the persistence slope; a mean-vs-median mismatch in the scale statistic, which made it worse at −0.254). The residual remains unexplained and is documented as such rather than patched.
- _2026-08-15 (session 29)_ — **The patterns→prompt feedback loop is off by default (`PATTERN_INJECTION_ENABLED`), and the burden of proof for turning it back on is *outcomes*, not accuracy.** Four consecutive production runs injecting the corrected pattern sets produced **1, 3, 2, 2** decisive calls (BUY+SELL) out of 63, against a **7–17** historical range, while over the same fortnight the median active ticker rose **+5.69%** and 67% rose more than 4% — the system stopped buying exactly as the market rallied, because every prompt carried `BUY es sistemáticamente fallido: 6% hit rate`, computed entirely inside the dead −4.80% May–June window. **The mechanism is hedging, not reversal**: both directional buckets drained into WATCH (22 → 28–30), so the model was not persuaded of a bearish thesis, it was persuaded not to commit — which is why the market-regime caveat never helped (hedging honours it). **The control was unplanned and unambiguous**: merging session 28 left the newest stored set without the gate's required schema fields, so 08-14 injected nothing and decisiveness recovered to **9 in one run**, no other change. The decisive point is that **the sets that did the damage were the most accurate the miner has ever produced** — corrected bands (s26), corrected cohorts (s27), corrected asset classes and a mechanical gate (s28). Accuracy was never the failing component, so no further accuracy work licenses re-enabling. **Mining deliberately keeps running and persisting**: the analysis value is real, and only the feedback into the prompt is harmful. The flag is written out as `"0"` in the workflow rather than left unset, so the state is visible in the repo. **Corollary — the |excess|-ranking slice pinned by sessions 27 and 28 is retired**: on the live id=7 set it ranks the collapse driver (`BUY … −2.4pp`) second, exactly as confidence does, so it would have prevented nothing. Ranking was never the disease.
- _2026-08-15 (session 29)_ — **A verification script belongs in the pipeline that produces the thing it verifies.** `scripts/check_run.py` existed since session 27 but had to be run by hand on the one machine holding the `.env`, which meant the Wed/Fri production evidence went unread for eleven days and three sessions in a row could not answer their own key question. - _2026-08-04 (session 28)_ — **A NULL `quote_type` is a wrong answer, not a missing one: `src.quote_types` overrides it for VHYL.AS and SPY5.PA.** The read-only stock-snapshots `tickers` table types 27 actives as ETF, 33 as EQUITY, 1 as INDEX and leaves **2 NULL** — both of which are genuine UCITS ETFs (`yfinance` reports `quoteType='ETF'`; their own names say "UCITS ETF"). For a fund that moves **1.28%** (SPY5.PA) or **2.05%** (VHYL.AS) a month against a stock's **15.87%**, stock bands are not conservative — they reproduce precisely the artifact s26 fixed everywhere else: HOLD is CORRECT when flat and these are always flat, so **70 of their HOLD rows scored a free CORRECT**. The override is applied at the three DB read sites (`get_active_tickers`, `get_outcome_features`, `_fetch_matured`) so grading, cohorts, the ETF prompt block and the sector bucket all see the corrected class from one place. **72 rows re-graded** (user-approved), all in those two symbols: 70 HOLD CORRECT→NEUTRAL, 2 WATCH NEUTRAL→CORRECT; 30d headline 60% → **59%**, `HOLD×ETF` 74% → **68%**. `resolve_quote_type` only ever *fills* a gap — a real source value always wins, so the map cannot silently contradict the table if it starts populating the column. **^STOXX50E is deliberately excluded**: it is an index, not a holdable instrument, so whether its calls should be graded at all is a different question from which band scale to use (carried — it is now the entire `(otro)` bucket, sitting at **+11.0pp** for the same mis-cohorting reason).
- _2026-08-04 (session 28)_ — **The excess gate is mechanical, fails closed, and excludes WATCH.** `select_patterns_for_prompt` now also requires `|excess_return_pp| ≥ 1.0` and `primary_action ∉ {WATCH}`, both new **required** fields on the pattern structured-output schema, applied **before** the top-3 cap so the cap chooses among patterns that already qualify. **Magnitude, not sign**, because excess is direction-blind (session 27): a rule like `excess > 1` would drop SELL at −9.4pp, the system's most skilful action. **WATCH is excluded outright at any magnitude** because it asserts no direction, so its excess measures which way the bucket drifted rather than skill — on live data `WATCH × (otro)` reads **+11.0pp** and would sail through a pure magnitude test. **Fails closed**: a pattern missing either field is dropped, so every pre-session-28 stored set (ids 1,2,3,5) stops being injected on deploy. - _2026-08-03 (session 27)_ — **The miner's market cohort is `(rec_date, ETF vs non-ETF)`, and excess return is direction-blind.** Excess = a bucket's mean forward return minus its cohort's, in percentage points. **Same day** because that is the market the calls actually shared; **same asset class** because session 26 established ETFs and stocks are not comparable instruments, so an ETF judged against a stock-dominated cohort would read its low beta as an absence of skill. Overall excess is **0.0pp by construction** (deviations from a mean sum to zero) — that is the anchor buckets deviate from, and a bucket sitting at ~0 is *definitionally* just the market. **The sign has no inherent meaning**: BUY/HOLD assert the asset rises, so positive excess is skill; SELL/AVOID assert it falls, so **negative excess is skill** — on live data SELL scores −9.7pp and that is its best evidence, not its worst. WATCH asserts no direction, so its excess only reports bias. This was found by validating against the live corpus before writing the prompt; the naive "positive is good" reading would have taught the miner to condemn its single most skilful action.
- _2026-08-03 (session 27)_ — **Telling the model how to weigh a statistic does not make it weigh it. Gate mechanically.** The mining prompt was given an explicit, unambiguous instruction — base patterns on excess, treat an extreme hit rate with ~0 excess as regime rather than skill — and a live validation call showed it **cited the excess in 8 of 8 patterns while still ranking by hit rate**: it labelled a `+10.6pp` bucket a "colapso catastrófico" for having a 0% hit rate, and praised a `−0.6pp` bucket as reliable for having 76%. The injected top-3 came out unchanged. **Standing rule: when a prompt instruction is load-bearing for safety, encode it as a gate on the output rather than a request in the input.** Concretely — `excess_return_pp` belongs in the pattern's structured-output schema so `select_patterns_for_prompt` can refuse to inject anything within ±1pp of zero. This generalises session 25's gate design (status + confidence + top-3) rather than replacing it, and it is why the `_patterns_block` regime caveat **stays** until the gate exists.
