## Current state

**Session 26 fixed a measurement bug that the feedback loop had been amplifying into production prompts** — **merged to `main` @ `c06c89b`** on 2026-08-03. It began as a production audit after ~2.5 weeks of unattended running, and the audit is the headline.

**Audit finding 1 — the grading bands were miscalibrated for ETFs, and every top mined pattern was an artifact of it.** `HORIZON_BANDS` are *single-stock* volatility bands (the 30d `watch_move` of 10% sits on the measured median 30d stock move of 10.7%) but were applied to every instrument. Measured: an ETF's median 30d move is **2.62%** (mean absolute 4.29%) against a stock's **10.70%** (14.67%), and only **13.6%** of ETF observations ever travel the 10% a WATCH needs to score CORRECT while **64.5%** sit inside the 4% neutral band that scores a WATCH INCORRECT. So `WATCH×ETF` was arithmetically required to fail — its 344 INCORRECT rows averaged a **1.66%** move — and `HOLD×ETF` was required to succeed, because HOLD is CORRECT when flat and ETFs are flat. The pattern miner had duly discovered both, promoted them to CONFIRMED at 0.91–0.95 across three Fridays, and **since Mon 07-20 the s25 loop had been injecting them into all 63 ticker prompts** to suppress WATCH on ETFs. The system was being taught a bug. `ASSET_CLASS_BAND_SCALE = {"ETF": 0.30}` fixes it: the factor is the measured ETF/stock mean-absolute-move ratio, which is **stable across horizons** (0.317 at 7d, 0.292 at 30d), so one factor serves every horizon and preserves the base bands' √time shape and monotonicity. New `bands_for(horizon, quote_type)`; `grade()` takes an optional 4th `quote_type` arg (default None → unscaled, so the index and untyped tickers are unchanged and every pre-session-26 test still holds).

**The re-grade (user-approved) landed and matched the pre-flight simulation almost exactly**: `WATCH×ETF` **15.1% → 59.6%** (vs `WATCH×STOCK` 67.4%), `HOLD×ETF` **94.5% → 62.7%** (vs `HOLD×STOCK` 50.7%), overall 30d **51.9% → 60.5%**, 7d 59.1% → 66.5%. The pathological ETF-vs-stock divergences are gone — they were the artifact. 4,299 rows re-graded in place via a new **`--regrade`** flag that drops the "not yet graded" filter and leans on the existing upsert (no delete, no id churn).

**Audit finding 2 — confidence is not informative, and that finding was itself masked by the band bug.** Pre-regrade calibration looked textbook-monotone (27.1% / 40.6% / 59.3% across <0.40 / 0.40–0.59 / 0.60–0.79) and had been mined as "Confianza 0.60–0.79: zona dorada" (CONFIRMED 0.89, injected). Post-regrade it is **flat**: 64.8 / 57.5 / 61.9 / 57.1 overall, and flat within stocks only (64.0 / 60.7 / 56.5) *and* within ETFs only (65.5 / 53.3 / 63.0). The monotonicity was an artifact of which action×type combinations happened to sit in which confidence band while ETFs were being auto-failed. **So all four top mined patterns were band artifacts.** Digest panel-11 will now honestly show a flat calibration curve.

**Audit finding 3 — `BUY: 2.6%` is mostly market direction, not skill.** Every graded 30d outcome comes from recs generated 05-17 → 06-26, a window averaging **−4.80%** (June −7.24%), with only 19.8% of outcomes rising >4%. SELL scoring 100% and BUY scoring 2.6% are the same fact stated twice. Measured *market-relative* (excess return vs the same-day cohort): SELL **−11.0pp** and AVOID **−5.6pp** are genuine skill, HOLD +0.9pp, WATCH +0.3pp, and BUY **−3.0pp** is mild real anti-skill. The miner's "BUY es un lastre: 0% en todas las muestras materiales" (CONFIRMED 0.88) wildly overstates a real but small effect; it sat at rank 5, one confidence reshuffle from teaching the system never to buy. **Feeding market-relative aggregates to the miner is the pinned next slice** (deferred by the user this session).

**Audit finding 4 — 2 of 8 scheduled runs failed completely.** Fri **07-17** and Mon **07-27** both hit `Batch … still in_progress after 2700s — canceling`: the client canceled and returned None for all 63 tickers, so zero recommendations were persisted and the job exited 1 (`run_metrics` rows 4 and 8 record `tickers_ok=0, tickers_failed=63`). Batch latency has a long tail — 07-20 took 37 min and 07-22 34 min, both near-misses. Fixed three ways: **`BATCH_DEADLINE_SECONDS` 45 → 90 min**; on timeout the batch is still canceled but then **awaited to "ended" (`_await_batch_end`, `BATCH_CANCEL_GRACE_SECONDS` = 5 min) and its already-completed requests are harvested**, so a slow batch costs the stragglers instead of the whole run; and the workflow's `timeout-minutes` **60 → 120** (it was already tighter than the batch tail — both failed runs burned 48 min). A test asserts the job ceiling stays above the batch budget so the two can't drift apart.

## Decisions log

- _2026-07-30 (session 26)_ — **Grading bands are per-asset-class as well as per-horizon: ETF bands = 0.30× the stock bands.** `HORIZON_BANDS` had always been *single-stock* volatility bands (the 30d `watch_move` of 10% sits on the measured median 30d stock move of 10.7%) applied uniformly to every instrument. Measured on 1,992 graded 30d outcomes: an ETF's median 30d move is **2.62%** (mean absolute 4.29%) vs a stock's **10.70%** (14.67%); only **13.6%** of ETF observations ever reach the 10% a WATCH needs to grade CORRECT while **64.5%** sit inside the 4% neutral band that grades it INCORRECT. So `WATCH×ETF` was *arithmetically required* to fail (its 344 INCORRECT rows averaged a **1.66%** move) and `HOLD×ETF` required to succeed (HOLD is CORRECT when flat; ETFs are flat). **The scale factor is empirical, not a knob** — it is the ETF/stock mean-absolute-move ratio, which proved **stable across horizons** (0.317 at 7d, 0.292 at 30d), so one factor serves all five horizons and, being uniform, preserves the base bands' √time shape and monotonicity (both test-enforced). `grade()` gained an optional 4th `quote_type` defaulting to `None` = **unscaled**, which keeps the INDEX and untyped tickers on stock bands and every pre-s26 test valid verbatim. **4,299 rows re-graded** (user-authorized, same precedent as sessions 06 and 14) via a new `--regrade` flag that drops the "not yet graded" filter and relies on the existing upsert — no delete, no id churn: `WATCH×ETF` 15.1% → **59.6%**, `HOLD×ETF` 94.5% → **62.7%**, overall 30d 51.9% → **60.5%**, 7d 59.1% → **66.5%**.
- _2026-07-30 (session 26)_ — **A mined pattern is a hypothesis about the *metric* until proven otherwise.** All four of the miner's top patterns — `WATCH×ETF es un lastre` (0.95), `HOLD×ETF es el guardarropa ganador` (0.94), `Confianza 0.60–0.79 es la zona dorada` (0.89), `BUY es un lastre: 0%` (0.88) — were artifacts of the grading definitions rather than findings about markets, and the s25 loop had been injecting the first two into all 63 prompts since 07-20. The calibration one is the subtlest: pre-regrade the confidence curve looked textbook-monotone (27.1 / 40.6 / 59.3), post-regrade it is **flat** (64.8 / 57.5 / 61.9 / 57.1, and flat within stocks *and* within ETFs separately) — the monotonicity was an artifact of which action×type combinations sat in which confidence band while ETFs were being auto-failed. **Standing rule: before acting on a mined pattern (or hand-writing a prompt intervention from it), check whether the grading definition manufactures it.** This retired the planned "ETF-WATCH prompt intervention", which would have hard-coded the bug into the prompts permanently.
- _2026-07-30 (session 26)_ — **Per-action hit rates are not comparable and are dominated by market direction.** Each action has different CORRECT/INCORRECT/NEUTRAL semantics (HOLD is CORRECT when *flat*, BUY when *up*, WATCH when it *moved*), so `HOLD 68%` vs `BUY 2.6%` is apples-to-oranges — and every graded 30d outcome comes from recs generated 05-17 → 06-26, a window averaging **−4.80%** (June −7.24%) with only 19.8% of outcomes rising >4%. `SELL 100%` and `BUY 2.6%` are the same fact stated twice. The honest lens is **excess return vs the same-day cohort**: SELL **−11.0pp** and AVOID **−5.6pp** are genuine skill, HOLD +0.9pp, WATCH +0.3pp, and BUY **−3.0pp** is mild real anti-skill. Consequence for the loop: **the injected block carries an explicit market-regime caveat** stating the rates are unadjusted and that the model must not infer an action is good or bad in itself (test-pinned) — a stopgap until `summarize_features` reports market-relative figures, which is the pinned next slice.
- _2026-08-03 (session 26 follow-up)_ — **`max_tokens` is a ceiling, not a charge, so generation limits get generous headroom.** The Friday mining call ran at ~2,900–3,000 output tokens against a 3,072 limit for three weeks, then truncated on 07-31 (`stop_reason=max_tokens` → structured parse returns `None` → the whole weekly mining silently discarded, leaving production injecting a stale set). Raised to **8,192**; the very next mining run emitted 3,135 tokens and would have truncated again. Any structured call whose output is model-authored prose should sit well clear of its ceiling.
- _2026-08-03 (session 26 follow-up)_ — **Batch timeouts must degrade partially, not totally.** The Batches API only serves `results` once `processing_status == "ended"`, and cancelling is not instant — so the old "cancel and return all-None" path threw away every request that *had* finished. Two of eight scheduled runs (07-17, 07-27) lost all 63 tickers this way. Now the deadline is 90 min (latency has a long tail: 34–37 min on healthy runs) and a timed-out batch is cancelled, **awaited to "ended", and harvested**. The workflow's `timeout-minutes` must stay above `BATCH_DEADLINE_SECONDS + BATCH_CANCEL_GRACE_SECONDS` — test-enforced, since the old 60-min job ceiling was already tighter than the batch tail.
- _2026-08-03 (session 26 follow-up)_ — **Weekly steps require a successful run.** On 07-17 a timed-out batch left `tickers_ok=0`, yet steps 10/11 still spent two Claude calls, wrote a `weekly_retrospectives` row describing a week it had no data for, and appended a `prediction_patterns` row.
