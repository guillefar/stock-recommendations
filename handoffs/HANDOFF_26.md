### 1. The audit (read-only, before any code)

Eight scheduled runs 07-15 → 07-29, 9 `run_metrics` rows, 4,173 graded outcomes, 3 `prediction_patterns` sets, 2 retros.

**Finding 1 — the grading bands were single-stock bands applied to every instrument.**

| | ETF | Stock |
|---|---|---|
| Median 30d absolute move | **2.62%** | 10.70% |
| Mean 30d absolute move | 4.29% | 14.67% |
| % ever reaching the 10% `watch_move` | **13.6%** | 52.2% |
| % inside the 4% neutral band | **64.5%** | 25.2% |

So **`WATCH×ETF` was arithmetically required to fail** — its 344 INCORRECT rows averaged a **1.66%** move — and **`HOLD×ETF` was required to succeed** (HOLD is CORRECT when flat; ETFs are flat). Ten days of prompts steered by a bug.

**Finding 2 — confidence is not informative, and the band bug had hidden that too.** Pre-regrade calibration looked textbook-monotone (27.1 / 40.6 / 59.3 across <0.40 / 0.40–0.59 / 0.60–0.79) and had been mined as "Confianza 0.60–0.79: zona dorada" (CONFIRMED 0.89, **injected**). **Finding 3 — `BUY: 2.6%` is mostly market direction.** Every graded 30d outcome comes from recs generated 05-17 → 06-26, a window averaging **−4.80%** (June −7.24%); only 19.8% of outcomes rose >4%. Market-relative (excess vs same-day cohort): **SELL −11.0pp**, **AVOID −5.6pp** (genuine skill), HOLD +0.9pp, WATCH +0.3pp, **BUY −3.0pp** (mild real anti-skill). The miner's "BUY es un lastre: 0% en todas las muestras materiales" (CONFIRMED 0.88, rank 5) overstates a small real effect and sits one reshuffle from teaching the system never to buy.

### 2. The fixes (user-approved after presenting the audit)

- **Asset-class band scaling** ([src/evaluate_outcomes.py](../src/evaluate_outcomes.py)): `ASSET_CLASS_BAND_SCALE = {"ETF": 0.30}` + new **`bands_for(horizon, quote_type)`**; `grade()` gains an optional 4th `quote_type` (default `None` → **unscaled**, so the index and the untyped tickers are unchanged and every pre-s26 test still holds verbatim). The 0.30 is the **measured** ETF/stock mean-absolute-move ratio and it is **stable across horizons** (0.317 at 7d, 0.292 at 30d) — one factor serves all five horizons and, being a uniform scale, preserves the base bands' √time shape and monotonicity. `_fetch_matured` now joins `tickers` for `quote_type`.
- **`--regrade` flag**: drops the `NOT EXISTS` "already graded" filter and leans on the existing `ON DUPLICATE KEY UPDATE` — upsert in place, **no delete, no id churn**.
- **Batch resilience** ([src/analysis/claude_client.py](../src/analysis/claude_client.py)): `BATCH_DEADLINE_SECONDS` **45 → 90 min**; new `BATCH_CANCEL_GRACE_SECONDS = 5 min` and **`_await_batch_end`** — on timeout the batch is still canceled, but then polled until `processing_status == "ended"` so its **already-completed requests are harvested** (results are only served once ended; that's the whole trick). A slow batch now costs the stragglers, not the run. All-None remains the fallback only if the cancel never settles.
- **Workflow ceiling** ([.github/workflows/run_recommendations.yml](../.github/workflows/run_recommendations.yml)): `timeout-minutes` **60 → 120** — it was already tighter than the batch tail (both failed runs burned 48 min). A test asserts the job ceiling stays above `BATCH_DEADLINE_SECONDS + BATCH_CANCEL_GRACE_SECONDS + 10`, so the two cannot drift apart.
- **Weekly-step gate** ([src/main.py](../src/main.py)): `weekly_ok = bool(all_recommendations)`; steps 10 and 11 require it. `--force-retro` / `--force-patterns` still override (ad-hoc backfills need them). One warning line when skipped.

## Learnings

Written at the user's request. The through-line: **this session's value came almost entirely from auditing production before building anything**, and every defect found was invisible from the code alone.

1. **A self-improving loop will faithfully amplify a measurement bug.** Session 25 built a clean, well-tested feedback loop; session 22 built a clean, well-tested miner. Both worked exactly as designed. Together they took a grading-band mismatch and turned it into confident Spanish instructions steering 63 real recommendations three times a week for ten days. **No test could have caught this** — every component was correct; the *metric they were all trusting* was wrong. When you close a loop, the loop's input becomes safety-critical infrastructure, and it needs the same scrutiny as the code.
2. **Ask "could my measurement produce this result even if the finding were false?" before acting on any aggregate.** The tell was there in plain sight: a 14% hit rate on observations averaging a 1.66% move. That is not a bad call — it is a threshold that cannot be met. Four separate CONFIRMED patterns at 0.82–0.95 confidence all failed this one question. Confidence scores measure the miner's conviction, not the finding's validity.
3. **Metrics that aren't comparable will be compared anyway.** `HOLD` is CORRECT when flat, `BUY` when up, `WATCH` when it moved — so their "hit rates" answer different questions, yet everything (dashboards, the miner, the retro) ranked them against each other. If you expose several numbers under one name, someone or something *will* treat them as commensurable. Either make them commensurable or don't share the label.
4. **A single market regime masquerades as skill.** The entire graded corpus is one −4.80% window, which alone explains `SELL 100%` and `BUY 2.6%`. Excess return vs the same-day cohort cut through it in one query and told a completely different, credible story (SELL and AVOID genuinely skilful, BUY mildly anti-skilful). **Any absolute performance metric over a single regime is mostly a measurement of that regime.**
5. **Fixing the measurement can invert what you thought you knew.** The confidence calibration looked textbook-monotone and was cited as evidence the model was well-calibrated. After the re-grade it is flat — confidence carries almost no information. The "good news" finding had been manufactured by the same bug as the bad news. Re-derive your reassuring findings too, not just the alarming ones.
6. **Degrade partially, never totally.** Two whole runs (126 recommendations) were lost because a timeout path returned all-None instead of harvesting the requests that had already succeeded. The information was sitting on the server. Any "give up" branch deserves the question: *what have I already got that I'm about to throw away?*
7. **Silent, marginal limits fail eventually and invisibly.** The mining call sat at ~95% of `max_tokens` for three weeks, then truncated — logged as an ERROR, but the run still exited 0 and the pipeline carried on with stale data. Headroom on a free ceiling is not a luxury, and "logged the error" is not "handled the error".
8. **Calendar-gated verification is a real failure mode.** Sessions 22–25 all deferred "verify on Friday" and each handed the deferral forward. The result: three Fridays of mining, two total run failures and ten days of bad prompt injection accumulated unexamined. The audit that caught all of it took under an hour. **Cheap periodic verification beats carefully-planned deferred verification.**
9. **Trust the repo over the narrative — and check the clock.** The handoff prompt was wrong about the merge state *and* about a cron being "shed" (it was merely 2h late). And I twice misjudged the date, once reading the local clock and once forgetting the user's reply came days later — which is exactly how two runs slipped in before the merge. `git log`, `gh run list` and the DB are the sources of truth; prose summaries decay.
10. **Small process slips, cheaply caught.** An ad-hoc script passed `(stats, mined)` to a `(result, stats)` signature and wrote a 0-pattern row; `git add -A` swept 21 embedded gitlinks into a commit. Both were caught by *looking at the result* rather than assuming success — verify writes by reading them back, and read `git show --stat` before pushing.

## Invariants (don't break)

All of HANDOFF_25's invariants stand **except the retired cron-shed one** (see §0). New or changed this session:

- **Grading bands are per-horizon *and* per-asset-class.** Change `HORIZON_BANDS` or `ASSET_CLASS_BAND_SCALE` in [src/evaluate_outcomes.py](../src/evaluate_outcomes.py), never at call sites. `grade()`'s `quote_type` defaults to `None` = **unscaled** — that default is load-bearing for every pre-s26 test and for the index/untyped tickers.
- **The 0.30 is an empirical volatility ratio, not a knob.** It is pinned by a test. If you change it, re-derive it from `recommendation_outcomes` (mean absolute forward return, ETF vs non-ETF, per horizon) and re-grade.
- **Any band change requires a re-grade** (`--regrade`) with user sign-off, or the corpus carries mixed semantics. `--regrade` upserts; it never deletes.
- **`BATCH_DEADLINE_SECONDS + BATCH_CANCEL_GRACE_SECONDS` must stay comfortably under the workflow's `timeout-minutes`** — test-enforced. Raising one means raising the other.
- **A timed-out batch must still be awaited to "ended" before reading results** — that is what converts a lost run into a partial one. Don't "simplify" the cancel path back to an immediate all-None return.
- **Steps 10/11 require `tickers_ok > 0`** unless force-flagged. A run that persisted nothing has no week to review and must not append a pattern set.
- **A mined pattern is a hypothesis about the *metric*, not necessarily about the market.** Before acting on one (or hand-writing a prompt intervention from it), check whether the grading definition manufactures it. Session 26 exists because that check hadn't been made: four CONFIRMED patterns at 0.82–0.95 were all artifacts, and the s25 loop had been feeding them into production for ten days.
